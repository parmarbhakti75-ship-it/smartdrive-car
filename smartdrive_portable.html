HC-05 3.3V Logic Level Protection (Voltage Divider Detail)

The HC-05 Bluetooth module chip runs internally on 3.3V. While its VCC has a 5V-to-3.3V regulator, its RXD pin directly enters the 3.3V microcontroller. Directly connecting Arduino Uno's 5V pin D3 will gradually degrade or instantly burn out the HC-05 receiver.

   Arduino D3 (5V TX) ───[ 1 kΩ Resistor ]───┬───> HC-05 RXD Pin (Safe 3.3V)
                                             │
                                     [ 2 kΩ Resistor ]
                                             │
                                            GND

1. measureDistanceCm() & Median Filter

Emits a 10-microsecond trigger pulse on pin A0. Reads echo return duration in microseconds with pulseIn(). Computes distance via formula duration / 58.2. Samples three consecutive pings and chooses the median to discard electrical noise spikes caused by motor brush interference.

2. Failsafe Watchdog Timer

Tracks lastCommandTime = millis(). In manual Bluetooth mode, if the phone disconnects or goes out of range for more than 1.2 seconds without sending an update, the robot cuts motor power immediately to prevent runaway crashes.

3. Autonomous Obstacle Avoidance Engine

Continuously inspects frontal distance. If under 30 cm: brakes, reverses 280ms, sweeps servo left (150°) and right (30°), compares clear paths, and steers into the wider opening. If both sides are blocked, performs a 180° spin-around.

4. Dynamic Active Braking

Instead of just setting PWM to 0 (which allows the car to coast forward under momentum), emergencyBrake() pulls both H-bridge sides HIGH, shorting motor coils through back-EMF to bring all four wheels to an instant dead stop.

Staged Commissioning & Testing Plan

Never test all components simultaneously on the floor! Follow these 4 rigorous engineering stages on an elevated test bench (wheels off the ground):

01

Stage 1: Motor Direction & PWM Polarity Verification

Goal: Ensure left and right wheels spin forward when commanded, and speed control works.

  • Prop the car up on a stand so all 4 wheels spin freely in the air.
  • Verify L298N ENA/ENB jumper caps are removed and connected to D5/D6.
  • Send command F via Serial Monitor. Check if both sides spin forward.
  • If Left wheels spin backward: Swap IN1 and IN2 wires (or swap OUT1 and OUT2 motor wires).
  • If Right wheels spin backward: Swap IN3 and IN4 wires (or swap OUT3 and OUT4 motor wires).
02

Stage 2: HC-05 Bluetooth Pairing & Failsafe Watchdog

Goal: Test wireless commands, smartphone pairing, and auto-stop on signal loss.

  • Power on car. Look at HC-05 LED: rapid flashing (2 Hz) means waiting for pairing.
  • Pair Android phone with "HC-05" (PIN 1234 or 0000). LED changes to slow double-flash.
  • Open Serial Bluetooth Terminal app. Send F, B, L, R, S.
  • Failsafe Test: Send F, then immediately turn off phone Bluetooth. Confirm the robot stops completely within 1.2 seconds!
03

Stage 3: HC-SR04 Distance Calibration & SG90 Servo Sweep

Goal: Verify accurate centimetre telemetry and smooth 30°-90°-150° panning.

  • Keep car stationary and open Serial Monitor at 9600 baud.
  • Place a flat cardboard box at 10 cm, 20 cm, and 30 cm. Verify telemetry reads ±1 cm accurate.
  • Check if the sensor reads zero or random 400cm spikes. If yes, check for loose echo/trig jumper wires.
  • Ensure the SG90 servo horn is aligned so 90° faces squarely forward.
04

Stage 4: Autonomous Navigation & Ground Obstacle Avoidance

Goal: Full floor run in closed obstacle arena.

  • Place robot on a smooth, non-reflective floor (avoid thick high-pile carpet).
  • Create a test arena using cardboard boxes.
  • Send command A via Bluetooth.
  • Observe cruise -> obstacle detection at 30cm -> stop -> slight backup -> left/right sweep -> steering decision!

Battery, Voltage, Current & Safety Engineering Rules

1. Common Ground Rule (The #1 Beginner Pitfall)

The Arduino Uno, HC-05 module, HC-SR04 sensor, SG90 servo, and L298N driver MUST ALL SHARE A COMMON GROUND (GND). If the battery ground and Arduino ground are not connected together, control signals will have no return voltage reference, causing random motor jitter, erratic readings, and lost Bluetooth packets.

2. Never Power Motors from the Arduino 5V Pin

DC motors are inductive loads. When starting or stalling, four TT gearmotors can draw 2.5A to 4.0A of current! The onboard 5V linear regulator on the Arduino Uno can only supply ~500mA before overheating and thermal shutdown. Power motors strictly from the battery pack into the L298N 12V terminal.

3. L298N 5V Regulator Onboard Jumper

The L298N board has a 3-pin 5V enable jumper:
• If your battery is 7.4V to 12V: Keep the jumper ON. The onboard 78M05 regulator generates 5V.
• If your battery is >12V (e.g. 14.8V 4S): You MUST remove this jumper, or the onboard regulator will overheat and fry!

4. Inductive Back-EMF & Reset Protection

When DC motors stop or reverse, their collapsing magnetic fields create high-voltage reverse flyback spikes. While L298N has protection diodes, solder a 0.1µF (104) ceramic capacitor across each motor's solder tabs to suppress high-frequency EMI noise that causes Arduino MCU resets.

5. Realistic Design Limitations & Disclaimer

This robot is an educational prototyping chassis:
• HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensors cannot reliably detect soft sound-absorbing fabrics, thin table legs, or obstacles approached at steep grazing angles (>45° reflection deflection).
• Skid steering on 4WD causes significant lateral wheel scrub on rough surfaces.
• Safety Notice: Under no circumstances is this architecture suitable for full-scale road vehicles or mission-critical autonomous transport.
